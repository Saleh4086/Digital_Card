Buying a Home in the East Bay: A Practical Start-to-Finish Guide

A practical guide to budgeting, preapproval, home search, inspections, contingencies, escrow and closing for East Bay home buyers.

By Sal Gharibyar, Broker/Owner · DRE #01418692

Published August 2026 · Expanded practical guide

This article provides general educational information. Real estate, lending, tax and legal circumstances vary by property and person.

Start with the payment, not only the purchase price

A realistic budget includes principal and interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, possible HOA dues, mortgage insurance, utilities and a maintenance reserve. A lender preapproval helps define the financing range, but buyers should still choose a payment that fits their broader household budget.

Buyers should compare the complete housing payment with normal household expenses and savings goals. A comfortable range may be lower than the maximum amount shown on a preapproval.

Compare neighborhoods and property condition together

Location, commute, schools, lot characteristics, insurance availability and future resale potential can matter as much as square footage. A lower-priced house may require substantial repairs, while a well-maintained home may reduce near-term costs.

Before touring seriously, organize income and asset documents, avoid new debt and ask the lender to explain estimated cash to close, rate assumptions and loan conditions.

Understand the offer terms

An offer includes more than price. Financing, deposit, inspection and appraisal contingencies, closing date, credits and possession terms can all affect the strength and risk of an offer.

Visit neighborhoods at different times and compare commute, lot, insurance availability, school boundaries, maintenance needs and resale appeal—not only square footage.

Complete due diligence carefully

Review seller disclosures, inspection reports, title information, HOA documents where applicable, insurance availability and any specialist reports recommended by the general inspector. Buyers should not rely on photographs or listing remarks alone.

Price is only one part of an offer. Deposit, contingencies, closing date, credits and possession terms can affect both the seller’s decision and the buyer’s risk.

Prepare for escrow and closing

During escrow, the lender completes underwriting and appraisal requirements while the buyer reviews disclosures and inspections. After conditions are satisfied, documents are signed, funds are delivered and the deed records.

A general inspection may lead to specialist reviews for roofing, plumbing, electrical, pest, drainage or foundation concerns. Read disclosures and title information carefully.
