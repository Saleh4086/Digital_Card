California Landlord Guide: Leasing, Maintenance and Property-Management Basics

Educational overview of rental pricing, screening, leases, deposits, repairs, notices and recordkeeping for California rental-property owners.

By Sal Gharibyar, Broker/Owner · DRE #01418692

This article provides general educational information. Real estate, lending, tax and legal circumstances vary by property and person.

Price the rental from current evidence

Use comparable active and recently leased properties while adjusting for condition, size, amenities, location and seasonality. An unrealistic asking rent can create vacancy and attract weaker applications.

Use consistent, fair-housing-compliant screening

Written rental criteria should be applied consistently. Verify identity, income, rental history and other lawful qualifications through a documented process.

Use a complete lease and move-in record

A strong file includes the signed lease, required disclosures, deposit records, move-in condition documentation, keys and communication history.

Respond to repair requests promptly

Owners and managers should maintain a clear system for receiving, documenting, prioritizing and completing maintenance requests.

Keep records and verify current rules

California and local rental requirements change. Owners should retain payment, notice, repair, inspection and communication records and obtain qualified legal advice when needed.
